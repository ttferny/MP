How SPF Works
  1. Mail server extracts the envelope domain from Return-Path.
  2. It fetches the SPF record from DNS (TXT record).
  3. Each mechanism is tested in order until one matches.
  4. The matching qualifier decides the result (pass, fail, softfail, neutral).
  5. If nothing matches, the result is neutral.

How to add this to your DNS
  1. Log in to your domain registrar (e.g. GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare).
  2. Go to DNS settings for your domain.
  3. Add a new TXT record.
  4. Set the name/host to @ (represents your root domain).
  5. Paste the generated record above as the value.
  6. Save — DNS changes can take up to 48 hours to propagate.
You can only have one SPF record per domain. If one already exists, edit it rather than adding a second.

SPF mechanisms & qualifiers

ip4 / ip6

Allows a specific IP address or range to send mail.

include

Trusts another organisation's SPF rules for third-party senders.

mx / a

Uses known mail servers or hostnames linked to the domain.

Qualifiers

+ passes, - fails, ~ softfails, ? stays neutral.

Before vs after SPF

Without SPF

  • Fake emails can reach inboxes.
  • Users may trust spoofed messages.
  • Phishing risk stays high.

With SPF

  • Authorised senders are recognised.
  • Fake senders are more likely to be blocked.
  • Overall email trust improves.
